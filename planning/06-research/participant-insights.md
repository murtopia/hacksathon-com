# Participant Insights

**Status:** Research  
**Purpose:** Capture recurring patterns and evidence from Hacks-a-Thon participants. This document supports messaging, product decisions, website copy, sales, and future product development.

## Insight 1: People overestimate the difficulty.

Participants often expected creating with AI to be harder than it was.

This matters because perceived difficulty is one of the biggest barriers to AI adoption.

## Insight 2: People need a clear place to begin.

Participants repeatedly encouraged future participants to start, jump in, avoid overthinking, and let the process be fun.

This matters because organizations often respond to uncertainty with more training, but the first step is often more important than more information.

## Insight 3: Personal ownership creates deeper engagement.

Participants were more engaged when creating something meaningful to them.

Some ideas were work related. Others were personal, playful, or passion-driven.

This matters because the project is not the only goal. Ownership is.

## Insight 4: The biggest transformation is identity.

Participants stopped thinking only like AI users and started thinking like creators.

Instead of waiting for someone else to make something, they began thinking, "I could create that."

## Insight 5: Confidence creates momentum.

Many participants left with more ideas they wanted to pursue.

Some continued using their projects after the event. Others immediately began thinking about their next creation.

## Insight 6: AI becomes a creative partner.

Participants described AI as something they collaborated with rather than simply used.

This supports the language of AI as a creative partner.

## Insight 7: Fun accelerates learning.

Participants described the experience as fun, playful, approachable, and energizing.

The Shark Tank pitches were especially memorable and culturally energizing.

Fun is not a side benefit. It is one of the reasons the methodology works.

## Insight 8: People are overwhelmed by the AI ecosystem.

People are not only overwhelmed by AI. They are overwhelmed by the surrounding noise: tools, models, podcasts, influencers, YouTube channels, newsletters, and conflicting advice.

This matters because people do not need more noise. They need a clear, guided place to start.

## Strategic Implications

- Organizations don't adopt AI. People do.
- AI adoption is a confidence challenge and a culture challenge.
- Personal ownership accelerates learning.
- Confidence comes from creating.
- Fun matters.
- Lasting AI adoption happens when people continue creating after the program ends.
