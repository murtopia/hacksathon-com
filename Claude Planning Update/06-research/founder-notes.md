## Founder Origin Story (raw, captured 2026-07-06 for the About page)

Nick's freestyle telling, condensed but faithful. Source for About page and future founder content.

**Agency years:** Launched Seven2 in 2004 with partner Tyler Lafferty. Later launched 14Four (second agency), and years later partnered with Ramsey Pruchnik to found Strategy Labs. Nick lives in Austin, TX; most of the Seven2 team is in Washington State. Murtopolis (murtopolis.com) is his one-pager venture tracker. LinkedIn: linkedin.com/in/murtopia.

**AI era begins (~2024):** Knee-deep in AI for 2+ years as of mid-2026. First 6-8 months: AI image/video generation, digital twins, avatars. Used most major tools. Left imagegen (no revenue model he liked) but still uses the tools. His son caught the imagegen passion with him and still builds prmptr.ai (prompt-building for image generation).

**The idea guy pattern:** Always brought ideas to the agency; the team worked on them in spare time. Been Watching (idea from 2019): team did branding, UI, and a little development over the years, but client work meant it never became a functional product he was satisfied with (last agency dev spring 2024). Classic "someone should build that" limbo.

**HyperChrono origin:** Dec 2023, back to lifting weights. Ended every workout with a 3-minute plank, recorded as a time-lapse for social. Wanted a stopwatch burned into the time-lapse showing true elapsed time over a 15-second Instagram post. Asked the team; no progress.

**Vibe coding awakening:** July 2024, discovered Lovable; started building HyperChrono himself. As a designer he wanted native Apple liquid-glass UI; Lovable made wrapper apps, not native iOS. Frustrated, paused. (HyperChrono pause #1.)

**Been Watching, round 2:** Early Sept 2024, tried building Been Watching in Lovable. Early mornings before running Seven2, late evenings after. Product too complex for where Lovable was then; roadblocks. Then reconnected with old friend Tony Rosland (photographer, heavily vibe-coding Studio Ledger, studioledger.app). Tony took him under his wing, got Cursor set up on his machine. Cursor was the game changer; incredible progress; months later a fully functioning product he shared with friends. Mobile-first web app; spent "way too much time on the polish, but that's part of what I really love." Still tinkers; may someday make native iOS/Android.

**HyperChrono deaths and resurrection:** Round 2: ChatGPT writing code pasted into Xcode for native iOS; countless hours; unsolvable errors; paused again. Round 3: Apple released agentic coding in Xcode; still couldn't get liquid glass working; bugs; gave up again. Round 4: early 2026, Claude Opus + Cursor tied into Xcode; Claude almost immediately solved the big problems. A couple months dialing in the app, marketing pages, user testing. Launched live in the Apple App Store ~March 2026. Note: nearly every logo/splash/art element started in ChatGPT but never quite good enough; manually rebuilt everything in Illustrator/Photoshop.

**The adoption problem, lived:** Getting the Seven2 team into AI was an uphill battle; most were resistant, nervous, or scared. Most clients at the time said no AI on their work at all.

**The accidental platform:** On a 4-hour flight to Spokane, built ~90% of the first IdeaLab (idealab.seven2.com) in Lovable on airplane wi-fi, including his first Google OAuth (locked to Seven2 accounts). Idea cards, thumbs-up, one-click AI competitive analysis and PRD docs. "A pretty valuable tool that no one used." Then: podcasts about "zero prompting" ("really should have been called one prompting") inspired ZERO.Prmptr.ai: an AI conversation producing the MD docs a project should start with (project brief, features, user flows, design guide, tech stack). Then noticed no good MD editors preserved formatting (Apple Notes was weirdly the best), so built edit.prmptr.ai (Google-Docs-feel MD editing). Also built other internal tools (ActivityRx.ai, fixes his Strava titles).

**The book:** Before the Hacks-a-Thon, started writing Vibe Coding for Creatives (~35% done, unpublished, may return to it). Thesis: agency/marketing creatives have superpowers for vibe coding: usability, design, UI, ideas. Most vibe coders are technical people who lack exactly that. This thesis helped drive the Hacks-a-Thon idea.

**The first Hacks-a-Thon:** Early 2026, idea to run it at Seven2 to nudge the team into AI and show them vibe coding is fun. Built a one-pager (hacks.murtopolis.com) outlining the blocks, including a link at the bottom telling people how to run their own. Realized the tools he'd already built fit perfectly inside it: IdeaLab as the idea repository, ZERO.Prmptr + EDIT.Prmptr for documentation. "At the time I didn't see it when I was creating those tools, but at the end these all culminated into something way more powerful than I could have imagined." Manually set up all meetings/schedules; conference calls matched the blocks. Shark Tank pitches were his favorite: costumes, rehearsed scripts (probably ChatGPT-outlined to feel like the show).

**The aha:** At the end, seeing the team excited about AI and building new tools unprompted: "That was my aha moment. This Hacks-a-Thon is a highly valuable tool to help companies get into AI. It's a little bit of a gateway drug." Then months building Hacksathon.com. "Super super passionate about the entire thing."

**Key insight for messaging:** He built the tools first and nobody used them. The event is what activated everything. Tools don't create adoption; the shared experience does. (The format is the product, proven in his own biography.)

## Notes

- AI is a creative partner.
- The best ideas still come from people.
- We help people realize they can create solutions they never thought possible.
- The Shark Tank presentations were some of the most fun Zoom meetings I've ever been part of.
- After the event, people built new tools without being asked.
- Layer two of the product is the culture hack: the participant experience creates the culture-building outcome.
- Shared creation leads to shared accomplishment. Shared accomplishment builds culture. Culture accelerates AI adoption.
- People do not need more AI noise. They need a clear place to start.
