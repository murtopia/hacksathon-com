## Page Strategy

Locked 2026-07-06 (Nick approved).

The job of Resources is trust and authority, not selling. The homepage sells, The Program explains, How It Works teaches, Resources builds trust. Long-term vision: the definitive resource on Organizational AI Adoption and the publishing engine of the company.

Structural decisions:

1. **Organized by the problem the visitor is solving, not by media type.** Four doors: I'm just getting started / I'm leading a team / I want to create / I want proof. People don't browse by "Articles vs Videos vs Downloads."
2. **Public name: Resource Library.** Nav label stays Resources (nav is locked). A library is searched and browsed, not read chronologically.
3. **The nine content pillars are internal taxonomy only** (planning and tagging), never public navigation. Nobody browses to a category called Psychology.
4. **AI Field Notes lives on Hacksathon.com, not Murtopolis.** Nick's call, and the reasoning is distribution: short notes published here feed X and LinkedIn with links back, building backlinks, SEO, and a visible pulse for the domain that actually sells something. Field Notes is content-engine infrastructure, not just voice.
5. **Small library with a pulse beats empty library with ambition.** Launch with the Seven2 story, 2-3 field notes, and 1-2 anchor articles; grow on the roadmap below. Same proof-gated rule as Built For verticals: a category appears publicly when it has content.
6. **No gated content at launch.** The ungated stance is a trust flex. When templates arrive post-launch as the single gated lead magnet, the contrast makes one gate feel fair.
7. **Resources must not delay the Product Hunt launch.** It is a post-launch growth engine with a minimum-viable launch state.

## Launch Content Roadmap

Eight articles, two per door, chosen for maximum value per hour of Nick's effort, plus the Field Notes cadence. Launch-viable state: Seven2 story + 2-3 field notes + 1-2 anchors; the rest fills in over time.

**I'm just getting started**
1. What Is Organizational AI Adoption? (the category-defining piece; own the category by writing its definition)
2. AI Confidence vs. AI Literacy (a settled internal distinction; mostly transcription)

**I'm leading a team**
3. Why AI Adoption Fails (executive thought-leadership anchor)
4. How to Run an Internal AI Hacks-a-Thon (best SEO asset: captures DIY searchers, shows the platform does it better)

**I want to create**
5. How to Pick Your First AI Project (the passion-project philosophy)
6. Giving Design Direction to AI (from the Seven2 research: the #1 friction point for creative teams; reference-site method, vibe descriptions, iterate-from-first-output, design-first tools)

**I want proof**
7. The Seven2 Story (already exists on the live site)
8. One participant story (edit from participant-reflections.md, not written from scratch)

**Field Notes cadence (ongoing)**
Formats: what surprised me this week / a workflow I can't stop using / a mistake I made so you don't have to / a tool that wasn't worth the hype / one prompt that changed how I work / what I'm seeing inside organizations. Twenty-minute writes. Each note syndicates to X and LinkedIn with a link back.

## Notes To Protect

- Resources builds trust; it never hard-sells. The only CTA is the contextual footer.
- No empty public categories, ever. Same rule as Built For verticals.
- Field Notes stays low-bar by design ("no polish, no thesis"); the format only works if publishing is nearly frictionless.
- Nothing from the original idea lists is lost: project-memory.md's resource ideas and the ChatGPT brain dump are absorbed into the taxonomy and roadmaps above.
- Murtopolis relationship: founder-journey content (HyperChrono, App Store struggles) lives on Murtopolis; when a piece teaches what the Hacksathon audience wants to learn, polish it into the Library. The brands reinforce, not compete.
