# Resources Strategy

**Status:** Working  
**Purpose:** Capture the content strategy for the future Resources section of Hacksathon.com.

## Role of Resources

Resources should become the long-term education and traffic engine for Hacks-a-Thon.

The goal is not to publish generic AI content.

The goal is to teach the market how to think about Organizational AI Adoption, AI confidence, and culture through creation.

## Core Content Pillars

### Organizational AI Adoption

Content that helps leaders understand why AI adoption is a people challenge, not only a technology challenge.

Potential articles:

- What Is Organizational AI Adoption?
- Why AI Adoption Fails
- AI Adoption Is Not Something You Roll Out
- How to Move From AI Awareness to AI Adoption

### AI Confidence

Content that helps explain why confidence is a stronger objective than generic AI literacy.

Potential articles:

- AI Confidence vs. AI Literacy
- Why Employees Freeze at the Blank Prompt
- How Employees Build Confidence with AI
- Why Creating Beats Watching

### Culture Through Creation

Content that connects Hacks-a-Thon to team building and culture.

Potential articles:

- Culture Is Built Through Shared Creation
- Why Shared Accomplishment Builds Stronger Teams
- The Culture Hack Behind AI Adoption
- How Fun Accelerates Learning

### Creative Teams and Agencies

Content aimed at the first target vertical: small and mid-sized creative agencies.

Potential articles:

- Why Creative Agencies Need an AI Hacks-a-Thon
- How Agencies Can Help Non-Technical Teams Create with AI
- AI as a Creative Partner
- What Seven2 Learned Running an Internal AI Hacks-a-Thon

### Proof and Case Studies

Content that turns Seven2 and future customer stories into evidence.

Potential articles:

- The Seven2 Hacks-a-Thon Case Study
- What Participants Said After Their First AI Build
- Why Employees Kept Creating After the Event
- The Moments That Matter in an AI Hacks-a-Thon

## Editorial Principles

- Lead with human transformation, not tools.
- Avoid generic AI hype.
- Use real participant language whenever possible.
- Support claims with proof from reflections, transcripts, or customer stories.
- Keep the tone founder-led, practical, and clear.
