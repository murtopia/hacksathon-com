## Positioning Direction (original working notes)

## Goal

Sell the vision: Hacks-a-Thon helps organizations accelerate AI adoption by empowering every employee to confidently create with AI.

The homepage should lead with the business problem and transformation, not just the hackathon format.

## Strategic Direction

People are not buying a hackathon. They are buying a faster, more human path to AI adoption.

The homepage should make the visitor feel:

- AI adoption is a people problem, not just a technology problem.
- Employees are overwhelmed and unsure where to begin.
- The AI ecosystem is noisy: too many tools, too much hype, unclear advice, and no obvious starting point.
- Hacks-a-Thon gives teams a clear, fun, guided way to start creating with AI.
- The result is both AI adoption and culture building.
- People leave with useful information, but more importantly, they leave with confidence.

## Core Homepage Thesis

Your team does not need another AI lecture.

They need a structured, hands-on experience that helps them build something real, understand what is possible, and feel confident enough to keep going.

Hacks-a-Thon gives organizations a repeatable way to turn AI curiosity into action.

## Hero Direction

The hero should speak to the adoption problem first.

Recommended direction:

**Help your team confidently create with AI.**

Supporting copy should explain that Hacks-a-Thon is a guided, hands-on experience where employees use AI to build real projects, tools, websites, workflows, automations, prototypes, or solutions.

## Hero Lines to Explore

- Help your team confidently create with AI.
- Accelerate AI adoption. Empower every employee to create.
- Give your team the confidence to create with AI.
- Turn AI uncertainty into hands-on momentum.
- The fastest path to AI adoption is getting people to create.

## CTA Direction

Primary CTA should be self-service.

Preferred CTA: **See How It Works**

Secondary CTA may point to interactive demos:

- Explore the Admin Experience
- Experience the Participant Journey
- View the Playbook

## Copy Notes

- Do not overuse hackathon language in the hero.
- Use tools more often than software.
- Avoid saying participants create AI-powered tools in every case. More accurate: participants use AI to create projects, tools, websites, automations, workflows, prototypes, or solutions.
- Clarify that people do leave with information, but more importantly, they leave with confidence.
- Include the overwhelming AI ecosystem problem: too many tools, too much noise, unclear who to trust.
- Position the event format as the mechanism, not the main thing being sold.
- Keep the Seven2 origin story as credibility and proof, then move the full story to the About / Case Study page.
